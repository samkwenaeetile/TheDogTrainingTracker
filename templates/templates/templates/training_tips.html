Dog Training Tips

Simple training guidance to help make sessions positive, consistent and enjoyable for both dogs and owners.

Use Positive Reinforcement

Reward behaviours you want your dog to repeat using suitable treats, toys, praise or attention.

Keep Sessions Short

Short training sessions can help dogs remain engaged. Dogs Trust recommends sessions of around five to ten minutes when practising training activities.

Start Somewhere Calm

Begin new training activities in a quiet environment with fewer distractions, then gradually increase difficulty as your dog improves.

Be Consistent

Use clear cues and a consistent approach so your dog can better understand which behaviour is being rewarded.

Record Progress

Keeping notes about training can help you remember what has been practised and review progress over time.

Know When to Stop

If your dog becomes tired, distracted or no longer wants to participate, take a break and return to training another time.

Common Training Goals

Recall

Teaching your dog to return when called can support safer and more controlled exercise.

Sit and Stay

Basic cues can help develop focus, patience and communication between dog and owner.

Lead Walking

Consistent reward-based practice can help develop calmer walking habits.

Important

The information provided on Dog Training Tracker is general educational guidance and is not a replacement for veterinary advice or advice from a qualified animal behaviour professional. If you are concerned about your dog's health or serious behavioural problems, seek appropriate professional advice.
